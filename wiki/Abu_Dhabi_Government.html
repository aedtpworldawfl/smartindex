The Abu Dhabi Government is the government of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi, the largest emirate and the capital of the United Arab Emirates.

It manages local government services, economic development, infrastructure, education, health, culture, and security within the emirate, while working alongside the federal government of the United Arab Emirates.

History

Abu Dhabi was ruled for many years by the Al Nahyan family. The modern government structure was formed after the creation of the United Arab Emirates in 1971.

In 1971, Abu Dhabi joined six other emirates to form the federation. Since then, the Abu Dhabi Government has led the development of the capital city, oil and gas policy, and national institutions.

The government has since expanded from oil-based administration into finance, tourism, culture, technology, and renewable energy.

Structure

The government is led by the Ruler of Abu Dhabi, who is also the President of the United Arab Emirates.

Day-to-day executive work is carried out by the Abu Dhabi Executive Council.

Abu Dhabi Executive Council

The Abu Dhabi Executive Council is the main executive body of the emirate. It approves policies, budgets, and major projects.

It is chaired by the Crown Prince of Abu Dhabi and includes chairpersons of key government departments and authorities.

Departments and Authorities

The government works through departments, authorities, and government-owned companies. Major areas include:

  • Finance and economy
  • Education and knowledge
  • Health
  • Transport and infrastructure
  • Culture and tourism
  • Energy
  • Municipal affairs
  • Environment
  • Security and justice

Economy

Abu Dhabi holds most of the oil and gas reserves of the United Arab Emirates.[#cite_note-1 [1]]

The government uses oil revenue to fund infrastructure, public services, and long-term investment through sovereign funds such as the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority.

In recent years, the government has focused on economic diversification through:

  1. Finance and investment
  2. Tourism and culture
  3. Technology and artificial intelligence
  4. Renewable energy
  5. Education and research

Capital City

The city of Abu Dhabi is the capital of both the emirate and the United Arab Emirates.

It is home to:

  • The Presidential Palace - Qasr Al Watan
  • The Abu Dhabi Executive Council
  • The Louvre Abu Dhabi
  • The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque
  • Major government ministries and embassies
Abu Dhabi is the political and economic centre of the United Arab Emirates.

Services

The Abu Dhabi Government provides public services through the TAMM platform, which brings many government services into one place.[#cite_note-2 [2]]

Services include:

  • Visa and residency services
  • Driving licences and vehicle registration
  • Business licences
  • Health insurance
  • School registration
  • Housing and land services
  • Utility payments

Vision and Strategy

The government follows long-term plans for sustainable growth, including the Abu Dhabi Economic Vision 2030.

Its main goals are:

  • A diversified and knowledge-based economy
  • World-class public services
  • Sustainable development
  • A high quality of life for residents
