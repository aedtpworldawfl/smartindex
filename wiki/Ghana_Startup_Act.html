What is Ghana Startup Act

Ghana Startup Act is a law passed by Parliament of Ghana in 2024 to support startups, innovators, and entrepreneurs in Ghana.

The Act creates a legal framework to label startups, give tax holidays, funding, and protection for young businesses.

History and Background

Ghana startup ecosystem grew fast - mPharma, Zeepay, expressPay, Farmerline, MeQasa, Shatta.

But startups faced no legal recognition, high taxes, no funding.

Ghana Hubs Network, NEIP, and Ghana Tech Lab pushed for Startup Act since 2020. Bill was drafted in 2021, consultations in 2022-2023, passed in 2024.

Similar to Tunisia Startup Act 2018, Senegal Startup Act 2019, Nigeria Startup Act 2022.

Key Provisions

1. Startup Labeling


  • Business less than 10 years old

  • Using technology or innovative model

  • Majority owned by Ghanaian

  • Certificate valid for 5 years

2. Incentives


  • 3 years corporate tax holiday for labeled startups

  • 50 percent tax reduction for 2 more years

  • Zero import duty on R&D equipment

  • Simplified business registration - 1 day via RGD

3. Ghana Startup Fund


  • 50 million USD fund to support startups

  • Managed by NEIP and Ghana Enterprises Agency

  • Grants, seed funding, co-investment

4. Innovation Support


  • Protection of Intellectual Property - easier patent at RGD

  • Incubators and Accelerators accreditation

  • Ghana Innovation and Entrepreneurship Agency to manage

5. Talent and Capacity


  • Startup visa for foreign founders

  • Training programs with GIZ, World Bank

  • Link universities - KNUST, UG, Ashesi - to industry

Beneficiaries

  • Tech startups - fintech, agritech, healthtech, edtech
  • Hubs - iSpace, MEST, Impact Hub Accra, Kumasi Hive, Ho Node
  • Young entrepreneurs - YouStart beneficiaries
  • Women-led startups - 30 percent quota in fund

Impact

  • Ghana ranked 3rd in Africa for startup ecosystem after Nigeria and South Africa in 2025
  • Over 2,000 startups labeled by 2025
  • Attracted foreign investors - MEST, Chanzo Capital, Injaro
  • Jobs for youth - addresses unemployment

Challenges

  • Fund disbursement slow - bureaucracy
  • Many startups still informal - not registered
  • Need more angel investors in Ghana
  • Internet cost still high for startups outside Accra
