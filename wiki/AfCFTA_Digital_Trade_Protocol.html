What is AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol

AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol is the first continent-wide digital trade agreement in Africa. It was adopted on February 9, 2024 at the 37th African Union Summit in Addis Ababa as part of the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA).

The protocol creates unified rules for digital trade across 54 African countries, covering e-commerce, digital payments, data governance, and digital identity.

History and Background

The AfCFTA Agreement was signed in 2018 and started trading in 2021. But it had no rules for digital economy.

With Africa's digital economy projected to reach 180 billion USD by 2025, leaders realized they needed a digital protocol.

Negotiations started in 2022. The protocol was adopted in February 2024 after 2 years of talks led by AfCFTA Secretariat in Accra, Ghana.

Ghana hosts the AfCFTA Secretariat in Accra, making Ghana the center of implementation.

Eight Key Pillars

1. Market Access for Digital Products - no customs duties on electronic transmissions
2. Data Governance - rules on cross-border data flows and data protection
3. Digital Identity - mutual recognition of digital IDs across Africa
4. Electronic Payments - interoperability of mobile money and PAPSS
5. Consumer Protection - trust in online transactions
6. Cybersecurity - cooperation on cyber threats
7. Emerging Technologies - AI, blockchain, IoT regulation
8. Digital Inclusion - SMEs and women in digital trade

Impact on Ghana

  • Ghanaian fintechs like Hubtel, ExpressPay can expand to 54 countries with one rulebook
  • Ghana.gov platform can integrate with other African e-government systems
  • Ghanaian SMEs can sell on AfCFTA e-commerce platforms
  • Mobile money interoperability via PAPSS - Ghana to Nigeria instant payment
  • Ghana hosts AfCFTA Secretariat - jobs and influence

Implementation

  • AfCFTA Secretariat in Accra leads implementation
  • Each country must domesticate protocol into national law
  • Pan-African Payment and Settlement System (PAPSS) is payment rail
  • Expected full implementation by 2026

Challenges

  • Different data protection laws - Ghana has Data Protection Act, others don't
  • Low internet penetration - 40 percent in Africa
  • Digital divide between North Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa
  • Need for 100 billion USD in digital infrastructure
