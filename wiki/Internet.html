Internet

The Internet is a global network that connects millions of computers around the world.

It lets people share information, talk to each other, watch videos, play games, learn, and work — no matter where they live.

The Internet is not owned by one person or one company. It is made of many networks working together.

What Is the Internet

The word Internet comes from inter + net — meaning networks connected together.

When your phone or computer connects to the Internet, it connects to other computers around the world.

You can:

  • Send messages
  • Make video calls
  • Search for information
  • Watch videos
  • Read news
  • Shop online
  • Play games with friends

The Internet works using data — small pieces of information sent from one computer to another.

How the Internet Works

The Internet works in 4 simple steps:

  1. Device - You use a phone, Computer, or tablet
  2. Connection - Your device connects through Wi-Fi, mobile data, or cable
  3. Server - Your request goes to a server, a powerful computer that stores websites
  4. Response - The server sends back the website, video, or message

Example:

  • You type aedtpworld.com
  • Your device asks the server for that website
  • The server sends the website back
  • You see the website on your screen
The Internet is like a post office for computers. It sends and receives information very fast.

Internet vs Web

Many people think the Internet and the Web are the same, but they are different.

  • Internet - The network itself, the roads
  • Web - The websites you visit, the cars on the roads

The Web is part of the Internet. Email, video calls, and apps also use the Internet, but they are not the Web.

History of the Internet

  • 1969 - ARPANET is created, the first network of computers
  • 1970s - Email is invented
  • 1983 - The Internet officially begins, computers use TCP/IP
  • 1989 - Tim Berners-Lee invents the World Wide Web
  • 1990s - Websites, email, and chat become popular
  • 2000s - Social media, YouTube, and Google grow fast
  • 2010s - Smartphones make the Internet fit in your pocket
  • 2020s - Video, AI, and streaming become part of daily life

Staying Safe on the Internet

The Internet is useful, but you must be careful.

  • Do not share your password with anyone
  • Do not talk to strangers
  • Do not click strange links
  • Do not share your location or personal photos
  • Use strong passwords
  • Log out when you finish
  • Ask an adult if something feels wrong

Think before you click.
