What is Drone Delivery in Africa - Zipline Ghana

Zipline Ghana is the world's largest medical drone delivery service. It was launched in Ghana on April 24, 2019 by Vice President Mahamudu Bawumia and Zipline CEO Keller Rinaudo Cliffton.

Zipline uses autonomous drones to deliver blood, vaccines, medicines, and medical supplies to remote hospitals and clinics in Ghana, Rwanda, Nigeria, Kenya, and Ivory Coast.

Ghana operates the largest Zipline network in the world with 6 distribution centers.

History and Background

Zipline was founded in 2014 in California to deliver medical supplies in Africa where roads are bad.

Rwanda was first country in 2016. Ghana became second in 2019 after government agreement.

First center - Omenako in Eastern Region launched April 2019. By 2022, 6 centers completed covering 90 percent of Ghana.

How It Works

1. Health worker sends order via WhatsApp or call 2. Operator packs product at distribution center 3. Drone launched via catapult - flies autonomously at 110 km per hour 4. Drops package with parachute at hospital - no need to land 5. Returns to center 6. Delivery in 30-45 minutes vs 3 hours by road

Distribution Centers in Ghana

  • Omenako - Eastern Region - covers Accra, Koforidua
  • Mpanya - Ashanti Region - covers Kumasi
  • Vobsi - North East - covers Walewale, Tamale
  • Sefwi Wiawso - Western North
  • Anum - Eastern Region
  • Kete Krachi - Oti Region - covers Volta Lake islands

Each center serves 500 to 1000 health facilities within 110 km radius.

Impact in Ghana

  • 2,500 plus health facilities served
  • 20 million plus medical products delivered as of 2025
  • Blood delivery time reduced from 3 hours to 40 minutes
  • Maternal deaths reduced - blood available for postpartum hemorrhage
  • Vaccine delivery during COVID-19 - 1 million plus COVID vaccines delivered
  • Snake antivenom, rabies vaccines now available in remote areas

Africa Expansion

  • Rwanda - 2 centers - since 2016
  • Ghana - 6 centers - largest
  • Nigeria - 3 centers - Kaduna, Cross River, Bayelsa
  • Kenya - 1 center - Chemelil - 2023
  • Ivory Coast - 1 center - 2024

Cost and Funding

Ghana government pays per delivery. Cost per delivery - 15 to 30 USD. Funded by Ministry of Health and Ghana Health Service.
