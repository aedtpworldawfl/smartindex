What is Africa Carbon Market Initiative

Africa Carbon Market Initiative (ACMI) is a pan-African initiative launched on November 8, 2022 at COP27 in Sharm El-Sheikh, Egypt. It aims to scale up Africa's participation in global carbon markets.

The initiative seeks to produce 300 million carbon credits annually by 2030 and unlock 6 billion USD in revenue for African countries by 2030.

History and Background

ACMI was launched by a coalition of African leaders including leaders of Kenya, Ghana, Malawi and partners including The Rockefeller Foundation, USAID, and SEforALL.

Africa contributes only 2 percent to global carbon market despite having huge potential in forestry, renewable energy, and clean cooking.

At COP27, ACMI was announced to correct this imbalance and ensure Africa benefits from carbon trading.

Objectives

  • Grow Africa carbon credit production to 300 million credits per year by 2030
  • Create 30 million jobs in climate sector by 2030
  • Generate 6 billion USD income for African economies
  • Ensure integrity and transparency in African carbon credits
  • Channel climate finance to Africa

How It Works

  • Forest conservation - REDD+ projects
  • Renewable energy - solar, wind, geothermal
  • Clean cooking - replacing charcoal with clean stoves
  • Regenerative agriculture
  • Mangrove restoration

Companies in Europe and America buy carbon credits from African projects to offset emissions.

Ghana Role in ACMI

Ghana is a key member of ACMI. Ghana's Forestry Commission and EPA are developing carbon credit projects:
  • Ghana Cocoa Forest REDD+ Programme
  • Clean cooking projects in Northern Ghana
  • Solar projects under Scaling Solar

Ghana aims to earn 200 million USD annually from carbon markets by 2030.

Challenges

  • Low price of carbon credits - 5 to 10 USD per credit
  • Verification and fraud issues
  • Land rights and community benefit sharing
  • Need for strong regulation
