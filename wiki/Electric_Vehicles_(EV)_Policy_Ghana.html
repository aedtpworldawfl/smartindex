What is Electric Vehicles (EV) Policy Ghana

Electric Vehicles (EV) Policy Ghana is Ghana's national policy to promote adoption, assembly, and charging infrastructure for electric vehicles. It was launched on December 2, 2023 by Ministry of Transport.

The policy aims for 30 percent of vehicles in Ghana to be electric by 2030 and to make Ghana a hub for EV assembly in West Africa.

History and Background

Ghana Automotive Development Policy was launched in 2019 to attract VW, Toyota, Sinotruk to assemble in Ghana.

By 2023, global shift to EVs forced Ghana to create EV-specific policy. Ghana has high fuel import bill - 2.5 billion USD yearly - EV policy aims to reduce it.

Policy was developed by Ministry of Transport with support from Energy Commission, EPA, and Ghana Standards Authority.

Key Provisions

1. Import Incentives


  • Zero import duty for commercial public transport EVs - buses, taxis for 8 years - from 2024 budget

  • Reduced duty for private EVs - 50 percent waiver

  • Zero duty on EV parts for local assembly

  • Zero duty on charging equipment

2. Local Assembly


  • Incentives for local EV assembly - tax holidays, corporate tax reduction

  • Kantanka, SolarTaxi already assembling electric motorcycles and cars in Ghana

  • VW Ghana exploring EV models

3. Charging Infrastructure


  • 1,000 public charging stations target by 2028

  • Charging stations at all fuel stations - NPA regulation coming

  • Private sector to lead - SolarTaxi, Grid, Bui Power leading pilots

4. Battery and Safety


  • Ghana Standards Authority to set EV safety standards

  • Battery recycling policy - to prevent environmental damage

  • Training for DVLA, Ghana Fire Service on EV safety

Companies in Ghana EV Space

  • SolarTaxi Ghana - electric motorcycles, cars, charging stations - Accra
  • Kantanka Automobile - electric cars and motorcycles
  • Wahu Mobility - electric bikes - women riders
  • Nana Electric - EV conversions
  • Bui Power - charging stations at Bui Dam

Impact

  • Trotro and taxi drivers can import electric buses with zero duty - reduce fuel cost by 70 percent
  • Ghana can reduce fuel imports and air pollution in Accra
  • New jobs - EV technicians, battery recyclers, charging station operators
  • Aligns with AfCFTA - Ghana can export assembled EVs to Nigeria, Ivory Coast

Challenges

  • Electricity cost - 1 kWh is 1.5 GHS - need stable cheap power
  • Charging infrastructure - only 10 stations in Accra as of 2025
  • High upfront cost - EV costs 2x fuel car
  • Battery disposal - no recycling plant yet
  • Grid capacity - ECG needs upgrade
