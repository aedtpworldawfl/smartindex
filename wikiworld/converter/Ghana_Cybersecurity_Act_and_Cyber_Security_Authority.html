What is Ghana Cybersecurity Act and Cyber Security Authority

Ghana Cybersecurity Act, 2020 (Act 1038) is the law that protects Ghana's critical information infrastructure from cyber attacks.

Cyber Security Authority (CSA) is the agency that implements the Act, regulates cybersecurity, and protects children online.

Ghana is now top 3 in Africa for cybersecurity after Mauritius and Tanzania.

History

Before 2020, Ghana had no cybersecurity law. Internet fraud (Sakawa), MoMo fraud, hacking were increasing.

National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) was created in 2018 under Dr. Albert Antwi-Boasiako.

President Akufo-Addo assented to Cybersecurity Act in December 2020. CSA officially started in October 2021.

Key Provisions of Act 1038

1. Licensing of Cybersecurity Professionals


  • All cybersecurity firms must be licensed by CSA

  • Ethical hackers, CISOs must be accredited

  • Over 1,000 professionals licensed by 2024

2. Protection of Critical Information Infrastructure (CII)


  • CII includes: Energy, Banking (BoG, GhIPSS), Telecom (MTN, Telecel), Health, Water (GWCL), Electoral Commission, NIA

  • Owners of CII must report cyber incidents within 24 hours to CSA

3. Child Online Protection


  • CSA protects children from online abuse, pornography, cyberbullying

  • Child Online Protection framework - 2019

  • Reporting portal - childonlineprotection.gov.gh

4. Cybercrime


  • Unauthorized access - hacking is offence - 5 to 15 years

  • MoMo fraud and Sakawa - criminalised

  • Cyberbullying, sextortion, non-consensual intimate image

  • Fake news that harms critical infrastructure

5. Incident Reporting


  • National Computer Emergency Response Team - CERT-GH

  • Public can report via shortcode 292

  • Email: report@csa.gov.gh

Cyber Security Authority Structure

  • Board chaired by Hon. Ursula Owusu-Ekuful
  • Director General: Dr. Albert Antwi-Boasiako - cybersecurity expert, Oxford
  • Industry Forum - telcos, banks, academia
  • Joint Cybersecurity Committee - National Security, Police, EOCO

Achievements

  • Ghana moved from 89% to 99.27% in ITU Global Cybersecurity Index - 2024 - 2nd in Africa
  • Licensed 100+ Cybersecurity Service Providers
  • Trained 10,000+ youth in cybersecurity under Ghana Cybersecurity Awareness Month - October every year
  • National Cybersecurity Awareness Month - Since 2018 - theme: Promoting a Culture of Digital Safety
  • Cybersecurity clubs in schools - KNUST, UG, Ashesi

Common Cyber Threats in Ghana

1. MoMo Fraud - Most common - fake calls from MTN, Telecel claiming prize
2. Sakawa / Romance Scam - Yahoo boys
3. Business Email Compromise - Companies lose millions
4. Fake News - During elections
5. Ransomware - Hospitals and companies attacked

How to Stay Safe - CSA Advice

  • Never share MoMo PIN - MTN, Telecel will never ask for PIN
  • Use 2FA for email, bank
  • Report to 292 immediately
  • Verify before sending money
  • Use strong passwords
