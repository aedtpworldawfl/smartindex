What is Mobile Money and Financial Inclusion in Ghana

Mobile Money (MoMo) is electronic money stored on mobile phone that allows Ghanaians to send money, pay bills, buy airtime, save, and borrow without bank account.

Ghana is now number 1 in Africa for financial inclusion growth due to MoMo - 70% of adults have financial account in 2024 vs 40% in 2014.

History

  • 2009: MTN Ghana launches MTN Mobile Money - first in Ghana - partnership with Ecobank
  • 2010: Tigo Cash (now AT Money), Airtel Money (now AirtelTigo), Vodafone Cash (now Telecel Cash) launch
  • 2015: Bank of Ghana releases new E-Money Issuers Guidelines - allows telcos to operate MoMo
  • 2018: Vice President Dr. Bawumia launches Mobile Money Interoperability (MMI) - managed by GhIPSS - you can send money from MTN to Telecel to bank account to E-zwich
  • 2020: COVID-19 - BoG waives MoMo charges for GHS 100 and below - MoMo usage explodes
  • 2022: E-Levy introduced - 1.5% tax on MoMo transfers above GHS 100 per day - affects usage
  • 2024: E-Levy reduced to 1% - GhQR and Ghana.gov.gh integration

How Mobile Money Works

1. Register at MTN, Telecel, AT agent with Ghana Card
2. Deposit cash - agent converts to e-money
3. Use USSD: *170# for MTN, *110# for Telecel, *100# for AT
4. Send money, buy airtime, pay bills, pay at shops via merchant ID
5. Withdraw cash at agent - small fee

Interoperability: *170# allows send to any network and bank account.

Key Players

1. MTN MoMo (75% market)

  • 16 million users - largest
  • MTN MoMo API - used by startups - expressPay, Zeepay, Hubtel
  • Services: MoMo Pay, QwikLoan, savings

2. Telecel Cash (10%)

  • Focus on SMEs

3. AT Money (8%)

  • Former AirtelTigo Money

4. Zeepay (Fintech)

  • Ghanaian fintech - cross-border MoMo - Ghana to Nigeria, UK
  • Founded by Andrew Takyi-Appiah

5. GhIPSS

  • Ghana Interbank Payment and Settlement Systems - runs MMI and GhQR
  • Owned by Bank of Ghana

Financial Inclusion Impact

  • 70% inclusion in 2024 - up from 40% in 2014 - World Bank Findex
  • Rural areas - farmers can receive payment via MoMo - Farmerline, Complete Farmer
  • Women - market women use MoMo to save - no need to travel to bank
  • Jobs - 400,000 MoMo agents in Ghana - many youth - your community has many agents
  • Government payments - LEAP, YouStart, NEIP pay via MoMo

E-Levy Controversy

Electronic Transfer Levy - E-Levy - introduced in May 2022 - 1.5% on MoMo transfers above GHS 100.

Government says to widen tax net and fund roads, YouStart.

Critics say it kills financial inclusion - MoMo transactions dropped 30% after introduction.

Reduced to 1% in 2023.

Startups say E-Levy affects innovation.

Challenges

  • MoMo Fraud - Biggest problem - fake calls, Sakawa - CSA reports 50% of cyber crimes are MoMo fraud - never share PIN
  • Network Failure - When light out, network goes down - your experience
  • High Fees - Sending GHS 1000 costs GHS 10-15
  • Agent Liquidity - Agents run out of cash in rural areas
  • Illiteracy - Some users cannot use USSD
