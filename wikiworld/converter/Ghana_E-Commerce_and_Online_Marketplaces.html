What is Ghana E-Commerce and Online Marketplaces

Ghana E-Commerce is buying and selling goods and services online in Ghana using websites, apps, and social media.

Market is $500 million in 2024 - small vs Nigeria $5B and Kenya $1B but growing 15% per year.

70% of e-commerce in Ghana is via social media - Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp - not formal websites.

History

  • 2013: Tonaton.com launches - classifieds - founded by Saltside - later bought by Jiji
  • 2014: Jumia Ghana launches - Rocket Internet - biggest e-commerce - sells phones, fashion, electronics - but struggling
  • 2014: Zoobashop.com launches - Ghanaian - but closed in 2018
  • 2016: Hubtel (formerly SMSGH) launches e-commerce and food delivery - Ghanaian
  • 2018: Social commerce explodes - Instagram sellers - hair, clothes, shoes - pay via MoMo
  • 2020: COVID-19 - lockdown - e-commerce grows 50% - people forced to buy online
  • 2022: Glovo, Bolt Food, Jumia Food launch food delivery - but Glovo exited Ghana in 2024
  • 2024: Ghana.gov.gh marketplace for government services

Major Online Marketplaces in Ghana

1. Jiji Ghana (Formerly Tonaton) - Largest

  • 5 million monthly users - classifieds - cars, houses, phones
  • Owned by Genesis Technology - Nigerian - operates in 5 African countries
  • Model: Buyers meet sellers - no payment on site - MoMo or cash
  • Revenue from ads - sellers pay to boost

2. Jumia Ghana - Second Largest

  • 2 million monthly users - formal e-commerce - like Amazon of Africa
  • Founded 2014 - Rocket Internet - listed on NYSE 2019
  • Sells: Phones (Infinix, Tecno), electronics, fashion, groceries
  • Challenges: High delivery cost, returns, Jumia lost $1B globally - closed in Tanzania, Cameroon
  • Still operational in Ghana but smaller

3. Hubtel - Ghanaian Leader

  • Ghanaian company - founded by Alex Bram, Ernest Apenteng, Leslie Gyimah
  • Started as SMSGH - SMS service - now e-commerce, food delivery, MoMo payments
  • Hubtel Mall - marketplace - food, electronics
  • Hubtel Food Delivery - competes with Bolt Food
  • HQ at Accra Digital Centre

4. Melcom Online

  • Melcom is biggest supermarket chain in Ghana - 50 stores - now online - melcomonline.com.gh - groceries, electronics
  • Delivery in Accra, Kumasi

5. Social Commerce - Biggest in reality

  • 70% of e-commerce - Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp sellers
  • Hair vendors - @hairbysiya, @wigshopghana
  • Fashion - @shopwithama, @kaysfabrics
  • No website - just DM - pay via MoMo - deliver via Okada or intercity bus - STC, VIP
  • No regulation - scams common

6. Others

  • Tonaton.com is now Jiji
  • Zoobashop closed
  • SuperYarn Market - groceries - Accra
  • Oja - groceries - Accra and Kumasi

Food Delivery =

  • Bolt Food - Largest food delivery - 2021 launch - 1,000 riders - Accra, Kumasi
  • Hubtel Food - Ghanaian - competes
  • Jumia Food - Part of Jumia
  • Glovo - Entered 2021 - exited Ghana 2024 - not profitable - high fuel cost

Payments for E-Commerce

  • Cash on Delivery - 60% of orders - people do not trust online payment
  • MoMo - MTN, Telecel, AT - 30% - *170# - most trusted
  • Card - Visa, Mastercard - 5% - low card penetration
  • GhQR - 5% - new - GhIPSS

Logistics and Delivery

Biggest challenge for e-commerce in Ghana.

  • No proper addresses - GhanaPostGPS helps but not used - riders call - "Where are you? - Come to the junction"
  • Roads bad - especially in rainy season
  • Delivery via motorbike - Okada - risky
  • Cost: GHS 30-50 for delivery within Accra - expensive
  • Intercity: STC, VIP buses, FedEx, DHL - GHS 50-100

Challenges

  • High internet data cost - your concern
  • Dumsor - light out affects sellers who need phone charge - power banks needed
  • Fraud - fake sellers on Instagram - Sakawa
  • No consumer protection law for online - buyers scammed - no refund
  • Trust - Ghanaians prefer to see product before buying - market culture - Makola, Kantamanto

Government Support

  • Ghana E-Commerce Policy - draft 2023 - Ministry of Trade - to regulate and support
  • Ghana Post GPS - digital address to help delivery
  • Ghana.gov.gh - government e-commerce
  • NEIP support for e-commerce startups - 2020
